# ReachPlan: bounded opportunity and alternative review

Research checked 2026-10-15. This is desk research from public practitioner, vendor and project documentation. No customer interviews, reference customers, adoption, production deployment or measured savings are claimed. Software was not purchased or exhaustively exercised during this comparison. Absence of a documented feature is not proof that a supplier cannot provide it, including through configuration or custom work.

## Supported opportunity

The proposed beachhead is a sewer-cleaning supervisor or small municipal contractor who already has verified, direction-labelled cleaning records and needs to prepare the unfinished part of a specific manhole-to-manhole job for review. The complete task is: normalize verified cleaned intervals into a single reference direction, merge overlapping coverage without double-counting, calculate uncovered intervals, compare those gaps with currently available endpoint access, operator-supplied usable reach and current barriers, and export draft setups plus the work requiring explicit review.

This narrow combined workflow is the product hypothesis. Generic sewer work orders, cleaning records, a remaining-footage total, interval arithmetic or hose-length suggestions alone are already well served. Demand for this particular independent CSV workflow remains inferred from documented operational requirements; it has not been validated by customer interviews.

## Practitioner evidence and its limits

* The City of West Vancouver's published sanitary asset management plan includes sewer-cleaning specifications requiring a reverse setup when the whole sewer cannot be cleaned from the initial setup. Its instructions also require operators to stop and seek a decision when pipe material or backfill is observed. This supports treating incomplete passes and later setup decisions as real workflow states, while leaving field judgement with the operator. [West Vancouver/AECOM plan, supplementary specification 02733](https://westvancouver.ca/sites/default/files/AECOM%20Sanitary%20AMP%20-%20Final%20Report.pdf).
* The Town of Hillsborough's cleaning/video project describes attempts from both ends and access manholes located in streets or private-property easements. This establishes that alternative endpoints and access constraints occur in actual commissioned work. It does not establish demand for ReachPlan. [Hillsborough project scope](https://www.hillsborough.net/DocumentCenter/View/229).
* Sacramento's scheduled jet-cleaning SOP describes progressive cleaning passes and judges adequate cleaning by the debris produced on successive passes. Consequently, a nozzle's furthest position or a CCTV inspection distance must not automatically count as verified cleaned coverage. ReachPlan must require that semantic distinction in its input contract. [Sacramento SSMP, scheduled jet-cleaning SOP](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/content/dam/portal/dou/utilities/reports/2018-2019-Sewer-System-Management-Plan-FINAL.pdf).
* The Coplay-Whitehall Sewer Authority's maintenance plan records cleaning/inspection findings in Excel, maintenance software and GIS. This supports CSV/spreadsheet interchange as plausible in this audience; it does not show that the authority experiences the particular interval-reconciliation problem. [Authority preventive maintenance plan](https://lehighcountyauthority.org/wp-content/uploads/APPENDIX-D-Coplay-Whitehall-Sewer-Authority-OM-Plan-and-II-Source-Reduction-Plan.pdf).

These are evidence of operational requirements, not endorsements, interviews or verified customer pain attributable to the named organizations.

Three independent search passes covered (1) generic sewer-maintenance/inspection products, (2) native and open-source linear referencing, and (3) hose-length, partial-cleaning and reverse-setup features, including German-language KANDIS material. No universal novelty claim follows from those searches. A customer already equipped with a well-configured KANDIS, WinCan or GIS workflow may have no reason to adopt ReachPlan.

The bounded rationale for proceeding is the reproducible composition of all the stated steps, with explicit contracts and unresolved-work output, for one independent CSV job. Its market value remains uncertain. If implementation only produces a coverage inventory or a nicer interval calculator, it has not met this rationale.

## Rejected discovery candidates

* Lift-station inflow/pump-capacity reconstruction from exported start/stop events: documented municipal spreadsheet work exists, but SoftMaid explicitly accepts existing SCADA event files and supplies cycle diagnostics, inflow, capacity and reports. An existing Missoula project also implements export-based flow calculations. General reconstruction was rejected. [SoftMaid](https://www.maidlabs.com/softmaid/), [Missoula notebook project](https://github.com/Oren-Fanok/Capstone-Stormwater-Flow-Rate-Model-for-the-City-of-Missoula), [Hutto practitioner study](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/publications/reports/contracted_reports/doc/1148311255_Hutto.pdf).
* Manual salt-spreader calibration cards: Cornell LTAP already supplies blank and worked calibration spreadsheets with the procedure. A new calculator/card interface alone was rejected. [Cornell LTAP resources](https://nysltap.org/nysltap-local-roads/publications-resources/online-answer-center/quick-answers/infrastructure/where-can-i-find-simple-chart-help-me-calibrate-my-salt-spreader).
* General operator rounds/daily water reporting: Waterly Rounds combines field/SCADA information, offline work, calculations and reporting. Its native documentation includes meter/tank/runtime interpolation. A generic rounds or daily balance application was rejected. [Waterly Rounds](https://www.waterly.com/rounds), [interpolation documentation](https://waterly.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/33000288465-data-interpolation).

## Selective application of Crossing the Chasm

Geoffrey Moore's own explanation defines a whole product around everything needed to satisfy the customer's reason for adopting, and describes a beachhead as intensive focus on a particular industry process. Applied here, this means completing one supervisor's CSV-to-reviewable-draft workflow, including installation, example inputs, unambiguous directions and units, validation, explainable output and failure cases. It does not justify asserting a market has been crossed. [Moore, “Crossing the Chasm: What's New, What's Not? Part 2”](https://www.chasminstitute.com/Blogs/tabid/288/Post/1694/Crossing-the-Chasm-What-s-New-What-s-Not-Part-2).

The Chasm Institute also treats competition as the logical alternatives a customer would consider. Here the market alternative is the supervisor's spreadsheet/manual cross-checking process; the product alternatives include an existing GIS analysis or configured sewer-management platform. There is no invented pricing, distribution, financial strategy or reference-customer claim for this open-source release. [Chasm Institute strategy overview](https://www.chasminstitute.com/WhatWeDo/eLearning/tabid/293/Default.aspx).

### Original two-sentence positioning draft

For sewer-cleaning supervisors reconciling incomplete manhole-to-manhole jobs who are dissatisfied with manually comparing direction-labelled passes and tomorrow's access constraints, ReachPlan is a cleaning follow-up planner that converts verified cleaned intervals into an unduplicated coverage record and reachable draft setups. Unlike assembling that task from spreadsheet formulas or a general GIS event workflow, it supplies a strict CSV contract, endpoint and barrier checks, uncovered-work exports, worked examples and an explicit review list in one local package.

This statement compares a specific workflow, not every capability of KANDIS or another configurable commercial system.

### Constructed usage scenarios; not customer observations

1. A 200-unit reach has verified passes covering [0, 70] from one endpoint and [120, 200] after normalizing a reverse pass. The supervisor receives the uncovered [70, 120] interval. If usable reach from the currently accessible endpoint stops before that gap ends, the residual work remains explicitly unresolved.
2. Two reports overlap on [40, 60]. Their coverage union counts that span once; the raw pass-length sum remains distinguishable from unique cleaned length.
3. A newly reported barrier lies between an accessible endpoint and an old uncleaned interval. Historical cleaning beyond it does not imply current passability. The draft must stop at the current barrier and retain work beyond it for review.

Usable hose reach, access availability, barrier status and cleaned-interval truth are operator-supplied facts. These scenarios do not establish hydraulic effectiveness, safe pressure/nozzle choice, traffic arrangements, structural condition, debris-removal arrangements or authorization to perform work. Those limits belong in the whole supported workflow, alongside the draft, rather than being hidden by a successful calculation.
